Reading the bars. The hatched band is the target zone; the red tick marks a tolerable upper limit where one exists. Micronutrient targets carry the vegetarian multipliers the plan uses — iron at 180% and zinc at 150% of the standard RDI, because non-haem iron and phytate-bound zinc absorb less well.
Where the error bars are widest. Tofu calcium: calcium-sulphate-set tofu spans roughly 200–680mg per 100g and Woolworths doesn't declare it, so the sheet uses the bottom of that range. Anything marked est is unverified end to end. Treat micronutrients as direction, not measurement.
ALA is tracked but does not count toward EPA+DHA. Conversion is real and the sheet estimates it, but the tracer fractions behind those percentages were measured at ordinary ALA intakes and do not extrapolate to 9g. Conversion efficiency falls as ALA rises, and linoleic acid — from the peanut butter, avocado and the canola in the hommus — competes for the same Δ6-desaturase. The empirical bottom line from feeding trials is that ALA raises plasma EPA modestly and DHA essentially not at all, and the effect is weaker in men. Your EPA is probably partly covered; your DHA is not.
Supplemental iron is meant to overshoot. Maltofer delivers 100mg elemental iron on dosing days and only 10–20% is absorbed. It sits far above the dietary RDI on purpose, because it's treating low ferritin rather than topping up a diet. Don't read that bar as excess.
On iOS. Everything works in Safari, but two things are worth knowing. Safari clears local storage for sites you haven't opened in about seven days, so export or copy your log at least weekly if you use it on an iPhone. And if the file is opened straight from the Files app rather than a web address, storage can be blocked entirely — the log bar turns red and says so when that happens. The Copy and Paste buttons exist because iOS sometimes refuses blob downloads; Copy puts the whole log on the clipboard, Paste reads it back.
Adding products later is safe. Snapshots are keyed by item id and store the computed macros, micronutrients and body figures alongside the selections. New products default to off in old days; a product removed from the library keeps its logged quantity, gets flagged in the log bar, and reappears intact if it is ever restored. The charts read the stored figures, so past days never shift when the library changes.
How the log works. Every day starts with nothing ticked. Body composition and locks carry forward from your last entry, since those persist between weigh-ins — but food, supplements and training do not, so a single tick can never commit yesterday’s whole intake as today’s record. Revisiting a saved day restores exactly what you logged. Data is held in your browser; Export JSON writes a real backup, and Import restores it on another device or after clearing browser data.
